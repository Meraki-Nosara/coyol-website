**Subject:** Inquiry Regarding Your Marketing Vendor

Dear [Client Name],

We are reaching out as a matter of professional courtesy regarding Scratch Agency, a Toronto-based marketing firm we understand provides services to your company.

We have no business relationship with you and make no claims against your organization. However, we believe companies should be aware of how their vendors conduct themselves, so they can determine whether such activities align with their corporate values.

**Background:**

A technical investigation conducted in 2025-2026 identified that websites publishing defamatory content targeting real estate businesses in Costa Rica shared infrastructure with Scratch Agency's domains.

**Key findings:**

1. **Shared IP Address:** The domain **nosarahills.info** (an attack site using the name of our legitimate development) resolved to IP address **206.223.180.218**

2. **Same IP hosted Scratch Agency domains:**
   - scratchagency.ca
   - scratch.marketing
   - scratchmarketing.com

3. **Coordinated registration:** Two attack domains (**marazulnosara.info** and **nosarahills.info**) were registered within 5 seconds of each other on May 14, 2025, and updated simultaneously on August 12, 2026 — indicating coordinated activity.

4. **Contractual link:** Documentation from a prior real estate transaction shows the email **david@scratchmarketing.com** was used by the purchaser now associated with these attack sites.

**What we are NOT saying:**

We are not making a legal accusation. Shared infrastructure does not conclusively prove authorship. However, the combination of technical evidence and contextual links is sufficient to warrant questions about vendor conduct.

**What we ARE asking:**

Does this activity align with your company's vendor ethics standards? We leave that determination entirely to you.

We are happy to provide the full technical investigation report and supporting documentation upon request.

Respectfully,

Concerned Stakeholders
Nosara, Costa Rica
