# Developer Solves Potable Water Crisis for 500+ Residents in Nosara's Rivera Norte

**A Decade-Long Journey from Health Crisis to Government-Approved Community Solution**

---

## The Discovery (2016)

In January 2016, while most developers focus solely on their own projects, Marion Peri took a different approach. Concerned about the health conditions in Barrio Los Ángeles de Nosara, he commissioned geologist José Vargas Gutiérrez (CGCR-180) to conduct a comprehensive survey of the community's water supply.

What the study revealed was alarming.

Walking door-to-door on January 31, 2016, the geologist documented **32 hand-dug wells** across the community. The findings were disturbing:

- Most wells were nearly dry — water tables ranged from just **4 to 30 centimeters**
- **Septic tanks sat only 5-20 meters from drinking wells** — far below minimum safety standards
- Several families had already confirmed their water was **contaminated with sewage**
- Residents reported stomach illnesses from drinking the water
- Many elderly residents and children depended on this contaminated supply
- Families resorted to bathing in the Río Nosara and hauling water in buckets

The community had no access to the ASADA Nosara, ASADA Santa Marta, or AyA water systems. They were on their own.

---

## Taking Action (2016-2017)

Rather than wait for government intervention, Peri acted:

1. **Purchased suitable land** (754 m²) for a community well
2. **Obtained all required permits** from Hacienda and water authorities
3. **Drilled a deep well** (now registered as **Pozo GA-334**) meeting all safety and sanitary standards
4. **Donated the entire property** — land, well, and infrastructure — to **Asociación de Acueducto Rural de Santa Marta de Nosara** (Cédula 3-002-345904)

The donation was officially registered on **March 8, 2017** (Finca 5-221215, Catastro G-1917568-2016).

---

## The Impact Today

The well is now fully operational, serving the Rivera Norte sector of Nosara:

- **65 services** currently connected in Rivera Norte
- **406 total connections** across the ASADA Santa Marta system
- **100% metered** for responsible water management
- Community health crisis averted
- Property remains permanently in community hands

---

## Government Validation: AYA Technical Study (June 2025)

Nine years after the original crisis, Costa Rica's national water authority (AYA) has now issued a comprehensive technical study validating the system's capacity and approving expansion.

**Official Report:** GSD-UEN-GAR-2025-02417 (June 25, 2025)

**Key Findings:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Current well capacity (GA-334) | 3.23 liters/second |
| Water balance | **Positive for 20+ years** |
| Required new storage | 169 m³ tank |
| Approved new capacity | 152.92 m³/day |

**AYA Conclusion:** *"El balance hídrico es positivo... la ASADA cuenta con capacidad hídrica para abastecer los servicios solicitados, así como a la proyección de crecimiento poblacional a 20 años."*

Translation: The water balance is positive — ASADA has sufficient capacity to supply requested services AND projected population growth for 20 years.

---

## Looking Forward: Infrastructure Investment

Following AYA approval, the developer is now investing approximately **₡81.6 million** (~$150,000 USD) in additional infrastructure:

- **New 169+ m³ storage tank** for reserve capacity
- **Upgraded pump station** with 7.5 HP submersible pump
- **Chlorination system** meeting national water quality standards
- **New distribution piping** (200m, PVC 100mm)
- **Perimeter security** for well site
- **Electrical upgrades** to national code

All infrastructure will be transferred to ASADA Santa Marta upon completion — ensuring the community maintains permanent control.

---

## A Model for Responsible Development

"When I commissioned that first study, I expected to find problems — but not a full-blown health crisis," says Peri. "Families were getting sick from their own wells. Children and elderly people had no safe water. Once we knew, we had to act."

The donation came with no strings attached. The land and well belong to the community forever.

Now, with government validation and significant new investment, the system is positioned to serve Rivera Norte for the next 25 years and beyond.
