Hi,

I have a story that might interest your readers — particularly for the Nosara newsletter and your coverage of water issues in Guanacaste.

**The short version:** A developer donated land and a well to ASADA Santa Marta in 2017, solving a 40-year water crisis for Barrio Los Ángeles. Nine years later, AyA just approved the system for 20 more years of growth. Now the same developer is investing another $150,000 to expand infrastructure — all transferred to community ownership.

**Why it matters:** This isn't a charity story. It's a case study in how developers and ASADAs can collaborate on shared infrastructure instead of competing.

The developer had options — private wells, separate systems. He chose to invest in the community ASADA because it was best for the village. The well serves locals now and will support future growth for everyone through the same shared system.

In a province where water conflicts are constant, Rivera Norte shows what happens when developers and communities plan together instead of fighting over the same resource.

**What I have:**
- 2016 geological study documenting 32 contaminated wells (families drinking water 5 meters from septic tanks)
- Property registry showing the 2017 donation (Finca 5-221215)
- AyA technical study from June 2025 approving 20-year capacity (GSD-UEN-GAR-2025-02417)
- Infrastructure investment specs (₡81.6 million / ~$150K)

**The angle:** This could work as:
1. An investigative piece on the 40-year government failure + private solution
2. A "Connecting Community" feature on developer-ASADA collaboration
3. A practical guide for other communities/developers facing similar situations

I'm happy to share the documents and connect you with ASADA Santa Marta or local residents if you want to verify independently.
